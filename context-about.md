# About Page — Context

## Purpose
This is not a generic "about the organisation" page. It is a storytelling experience about **Baba Deep Singh Ji** — his life, his courage, and his martyrdom. The goal is to make someone feel the weight of his story, not just read facts.

## What "Chaupehra" Means
- Derived from "chau" (four) + "pehra" (watch/guard duty)
- Means "four watches of protection" — a vigil of prayer spanning the day
- Rooted in devotional practice at Shaheedan Sahib Gurdwara, Amritsar — connected directly to Baba Deep Singh Ji
- In Canada (Surrey, Brampton), Chaupehra Sahib is a community prayer program held on weekends
- The name evokes: standing guard, spiritual vigil, community gathering, and protection — all themes of his life

## Storytelling Structure (6 Acts)

### Act 1 — The Lamp is Born
- Born January 1682, Pahuwind village, Amritsar district, Punjab
- Parents: Bhai Bhagta Ji & Mata Jeoni Ji — devout Sikh farmers, Sandhu Jat clan
- A saint foretold: "Your son will be a lamp to the world." They named him Deep — meaning light.
- At age 12 (~1694), he traveled with his parents to Anandpur Sahib to see Guru Gobind Singh Ji
- The Guru asked him to stay. The boy accepted. He never really went home.

### Act 2 — Forged at Anandpur
- 6-8 years at the Guru's side during the most turbulent period in Sikh history
- Trained simultaneously in: Gurbani (scripture), martial arts, horseback riding, weaponry
- Received Khande-di-Pahul (Khalsa initiation) at Vaisakhi 1700, age ~18 — from Guru Gobind Singh Ji himself
- Studied under Bhai Mani Singh Ji — the greatest Sikh scholar of the era

### Act 3 — The Scribe of Scripture
- 1705-06: After the Guru lost his four sons, his mother, and thousands of Sikhs, he arrived at Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda district) — exhausted and in exile
- The Guru recited the entire Guru Granth Sahib from memory. Baba Deep Singh Ji and Bhai Mani Singh Ji wrote every word by hand.
- This became the Damdami Bir — the authoritative final text of the Guru Granth Sahib
- He then handwrote four more copies — one for each of the four Takhts (the supreme seats of Sikh authority)
- The Guru appointed him head of Damdama Sahib before departing. He held that post for 50+ years.
- Damdama Sahib under his stewardship became the premier Sikh center of learning — the Damdami Taksal (the Mint of Damdama). It continues today.

### Act 4 — The Saint-Soldier
- Led the Shaheed Misl (the Martyrs' Confederacy), appointed first head at Vaisakhi 1748, age 66
- Mandate of the Misl: protect Sikh shrines and uphold the tradition of sacrifice
- 1757 — Ahmad Shah Durrani's 4th invasion of India swept through Punjab with thousands of civilian captives
- Near Kurukshetra, Baba Deep Singh Ji's forces launched a surprise attack on the caravan
- Freed approximately 300 women and 100 young men, escorted them to safety
- Durrani, enraged, ordered retaliation: Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple) was attacked, its sarovar (sacred pool) filled with animal entrails, and its structure demolished

### Act 5 — The Vow (The Line in the Earth)
- News of the desecration reached Damdama Sahib. He was 75 years old.
- He lifted his 14-kg double-edged Khanda and prayed: **"Sir jaave ta jaave, mera Sikhi sidhak na jaave"** — *"If my head goes, let it go — but do not let my Sikhi waver."*
- His specific Ardas: *"May my head fall at Sri Harmandir Sahib."*
- Word spread through the villages. By the time he reached Tarn Taran — 10 miles from Amritsar — 5,000 Sikhs had joined him
- He stopped. He drew a line in the earth with his sword. He told them the truth: **an army of 20,000 stands ahead. Cross this line only if you have accepted death.**
- Every single person crossed. **Gurdwara Lakir Sahib marks that exact spot today.**

### Act 6 — The Final March
- Battle of Gohalwar (village of Chabba, outskirts of Amritsar) — November 11, 1757
- An Afghan commander struck a devastating blow, severing Baba Deep Singh Ji's head
- A Sikh companion called out: *"Remember your Ardas — your head must fall at Harmandir Sahib."*
- He raised his severed head on his left palm. He advanced with his Khanda in his right hand.
- He reached the parkarma — the circumambulatory path of Darbar Sahib — and fell.
- His vow was kept. **A stone marker is embedded in the parkarma floor at that exact spot.**
- His Khanda is preserved at Sri Akal Takht Sahib — the supreme seat of Sikh authority — to this day.
- Age at martyrdom: **75 years.**

## Design Direction for About Page

### Scrollytelling Layout
- Full-width sections, one act per viewport section
- **Parallax background images** — historic paintings, Gurdwara photography, illustrations — scroll in the background as the user reads
- Text in a centered, readable column on top of the imagery (with dark overlay for contrast)
- Each section has a **bold act title** + body copy
- Quote callouts in Punjabi/Gurmukhi for the key utterances (with English translation below)

### Image Suggestions (one per act or between each)
1. Portrait of Baba Deep Singh Ji as a young man / Pahuwind village scene
2. Anandpur Sahib historical illustration or photograph
3. Damdama Sahib Gurdwara / handwritten Guru Granth Sahib manuscript
4. Illustration of the rescue of captives / battle scene
5. The iconic image: Baba Deep Singh Ji drawing the line at Tarn Taran
6. **The iconic martyr portrait: severed head on palm, Khanda raised** — most powerful image, used as the climactic visual

### Key Quotes (Bilingual — always show both)
- **"Sir jaave ta jaave, mera Sikhi sidhak na jaave"**
  *"If my head goes, let it go — but do not let my Sikhi waver."*

### Typography
- Section headings: large, bold, orange (`#FF6600` or Sikh saffron)
- Body: 18-20px, high contrast on dark backgrounds
- Gurmukhi quotes: slightly larger, a dedicated Punjabi web font (Noto Sans Gurmukhi)

### Tone
- Reverent, not academic
- Active voice, present tense where possible ("He lifts his Khanda. He draws a line.")
- No flowery or corporate language
- Suitable for a 65-year-old Sikh reader — familiar with the story but wanting to feel it again
